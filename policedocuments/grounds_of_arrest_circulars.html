1. DGP Circular Memorandum – 06 November 2026

DocumentRc. No. 15565535/Crime 3(2)/2026, dated 06.11.2026
SubjectArrest procedure – communication of grounds of arrest – directions of the Madras High Court
Court referenceMadras High Court, Crl.O.P. No. 22765 of 2026, order dated 07.09.2026
Earlier referenceChief Office Circular Memorandum Rc. No. 12244800/Crime-4(3)/2025, dated 23.11.2025
Original scanned PDF pages1–3: circular memorandum; 4–6: enclosed form

Instructions highlighted in the memorandum

  1. Record the factual, case-specific grounds of arrest, the material on which the arrest is founded, and the offence-wise reasons. Simply reproducing printed statutory wording is not sufficient.
  2. Separately record the need and necessity for arrest against the relevant circumstances in Section 35(1)(b)(ii) BNSS. Mechanical repetition of statutory heads is to be discontinued.
  3. Furnish the recorded grounds in writing to the arrested person, preferably in a language the person understands, within the time directed by the courts.
  4. Effect and record arrest intimation to the relative, friend or nominated person required by Section 48 BNSS. Record the particulars and timing, so compliance can be shown before the court.
  5. Circulate and use the revised prescribed format across units, including specialised units, with acknowledgment of the instructions.
  6. Where the arrested person is not familiar with Tamil or English, read and explain the grounds in a language known to the person, record acknowledgement of translation, and obtain a witness to that process as instructed.

2. Prescribed Grounds of Arrest Form

Enclosure in the 2026 memorandum – scanned pages 4–6. The form is bilingual (English/Tamil) and provides for:

  • Police station, crime number, and acts/sections;
  • Name, father/husband/guardian name, address and mobile number of arrested person;
  • Date/time/place of arrest, date/time/place of communication of grounds, and name/designation of arresting officer;
  • 1. Gist of the FIR: one-paragraph facts; for drug seizure cases, specify exact weight of contraband;
  • 2. Accusation and role: precise allegations against the arrested person and search/seizure details, including articles seized, where applicable;
  • 3. Reasons and necessity for arrest: specific facts supporting the relevant Section 35(1)(b)(ii) BNSS heads;
  • Signature of arrested person; signature and seal of arresting officer; witness acknowledgement in a translation case.

3. Earlier DGP Circular – 23 November 2025

DocumentRc. No. 12244800/Crime-4(3)/2025, dated 23.11.2025
Court referenceSupreme Court of India, Criminal Appeal No. 2195 of 2025, order dated 06.11.2025
Original scanned PDF pages7–9: 2025 circular; 11–12: Public Prosecutor's covering communication (dated 10.11.2025). Page 10 is blank.

Principal directions summarised

  1. The constitutional obligation to communicate grounds of arrest applies to offences under the IPC and BNS, among other offences.
  2. Grounds of arrest must be communicated in writing in a language understood by the arrestee.
  3. In exceptional circumstances where written grounds cannot be handed over at or immediately after arrest, oral communication may initially occur. Written grounds must then be furnished within a reasonable time and, in any event, at least two hours before production for remand, as set out in the circular.
  4. Police should record the person nominated by the arrested person (relative, friend or other person) and inform that person as required, including of the reasons and place of custody.
  5. Failure to communicate the written grounds in compliance with the directions risks rendering arrest and remand illegal; the circular directs strict compliance and warns of departmental action.

Practical arrest-documentation checklist

  1. Verify the offence, provisions invoked, FIR facts and available evidence against this particular person.
  2. Prepare individualized written grounds and independently articulate arrest necessity under the applicable BNSS provisions.
  3. Record the arrest date, time and place, and the arresting officer's name/designation.
  4. Give the arrested person the grounds in a language understood; where translated, record the explanation and witness acknowledgement.
  5. Take acknowledgement of service and record exact date, time, location and any refusal to acknowledge.
  6. Notify the nominated person/relative/friend as required and preserve proof of notification.
  7. Check remand-production timing and confirm written grounds have been furnished within the prescribed period.
  8. Place supporting records of communication and necessity in the appropriate case/remand file.
Important: This checklist is a quick reference. It is not legal advice, not a substitute for the authenticated circular and its exact annexure, and not a legal finding in any particular case.
